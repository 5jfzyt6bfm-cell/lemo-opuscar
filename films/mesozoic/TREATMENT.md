# How Long Would You Last? — treatment

Style: Art Deco (`styles/art-deco/STYLE.md`). 30 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps, English voice-over + burned-in subtitles, −14 LUFS.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The exhibition, three pavilions.** Three gold doors — Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous — each opens on one hazard. Clear, but three worlds in 30 s means three set-ups and no time to feel any of them.
2. **The trophy night.** Hazards as nominees on sunburst panels ("Best Killer: Heat / Thirst / Teeth"), the winner lit in bulbs. Fun, but the viewer never *goes* there; it stays a list.
3. **The survival dial (chosen).** One traveller, one door, one gold clock that is the plot. The dial starts at "?" and is driven down by each fact, then wound back up by one human act (a fire). It is a single journey with a turn, it uses the style's strongest native move (*dials carry the plot*) and it answers the question with a number the audience can read.

Opening image: a gold date dial engraving itself and spinning backwards (the question as a machine). Ending: the doors close and the survival dial rhymes into a far larger epoch medallion — 165 million years — "the lights go out" until one bulb (you) remains.

## 2. Logline and arc

A time traveller steps 200 million years back, and a gold dial counts how long they'd last: not the air, not the dinosaurs — thirst is the clock.

- **Setup** (0–8 s): the question; the door opens on the Triassic–Jurassic world. Expectation: you'd die fast.
- **Turn 1** (8–13 s): good news — you can breathe (thin air, like a mountain town).
- **Turn 2** (13–21 s): the heat and the water: the dial drops to **3 DAYS**.
- **Turn 3** (21–26 s): fire, shade, boiled water — the dial unwinds upward: *maybe years*.
- **Ending** (26–30 s): scale reveal — the dinosaurs' dial reads 165 million years. Lights go out to one bulb.

## 3. Benchmark

- *Cassandre's travel posters* (grammar): giant geometry, steep perspective, airbrush inside hard edges. Learn: one huge shape per frame. Don't take: any layout.
- *Walking with Dinosaurs* (1999), survival framing. Learn: one concrete stake per scene, told as a documentary fact. Don't take: designs, creatures, music.
- *1939 World's Fair dioramas*. Learn: the world as a staged, centred set behind a proscenium. Don't take: any real building.

## 6. Cue map

Foxtrot, 112 bpm (beat = 0.536 s), 2/4 feel. Instruments: stride piano, muted trumpet, pizz bass, brushes, tubular bell, harp gliss, celesta.

- 0–3.5 intro: harp gliss up on the date spin; bell on the lock at 3.2.
- 3.5–8.0 A: brushes + pizz bass enter on the door split (3.5).
- 8–13 A': piano melody fragment; trumpet answer under "won't kill you".
- 13–17.5 B: low strings tremolo, bass pedal, rising — heat.
- 17.5–18.3 **silence**, clock tick only. 18.3 hands spin; bulbs on beats at 20.0 / 20.5 / 21.0 (one hit each).
- 21.5–26 C: full theme (planted in fragments) — the turn. Clock unwinds with a xylophone run.
- 26–30 coda: **near-silence** before the epoch medallion lands (26.6, bell + low piano chord); bulbs click out in reverse; last bulb hums; end.

- Subtitle card (`subtitleCard`): lacquer bar with stepped ends, double gold keyline, Josefin Sans SemiBold ~40 px ivory, unfolds from the centre; lower third, never over the dial.
- Titles: Limelight. Numbers on dials: Poiret One / Italiana. Dial labels: Josefin Sans.
- Palette: warm black ground, gold, ivory; **coral** accent for the traveller and the fire only; **emerald** jewel for the Mesozoic sky near the horizon and foliage.
