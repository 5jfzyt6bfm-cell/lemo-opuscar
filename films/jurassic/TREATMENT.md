# One Week in the Jurassic — treatment

Style: Art Deco (`styles/art-deco/STYLE.md`). The user's script and voiceover (ElevenLabs, voice "Peter", three files, ≈ 10 min 56 s of speech), 1920×1080, 24 fps, English, burned-in subtitles + `.srt`, −14 LUFS. The user asked for humans and dinosaurs, with **dinosaurs that look like the real animals**: within the deco look (airbrushed volumes, gold keylines), every dinosaur is drawn from published skeletal proportions in metres: posture, skull shape, limb ratios, plates and spikes as the fossils show them. No dragging tails, no upright "Godzilla" theropods, no pronated hands.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The museum hall.** Each section of the script is a gallery behind a deco door: Air, Water, Food, Giants, Predators, Infection. Clean, but a 10-minute walk through galleries keeps the soldiers at a distance. It becomes a lecture.
2. **The field diary.** A soldier's notebook in gold engraving, page by page. Intimate, but the script is told by a narrator, not a soldier, and a notebook flattens the big animals.
3. **The seven-day clock and the threat board (chosen).** Two machines carry the plot, as the style's "dials carry the plot" move: a gold **DAY medallion** that turns 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 5 → 7, and a **threat board**, a row of enamel plaques (AIR · WATER · FOOD · GIANTS · PREDATORS · EXHAUSTION · INFECTION) that lights one bulb at a time as the script answers "which part of this world would kill them first". Between them the film lives in the world: the floodplain, the river camp, the animals at full scale beside the soldiers. Over 11 minutes the audience always knows where they are (the day) and what is being weighed (the plaque).

The opening is silence, not a title: one gold fern drawing itself on black, with wind. The ending: the soldiers leave through deco doors, the world carries on, and the last shot pulls down through the ground into the rock where the same animals lie as bones. That's the "Utah and Wyoming" line made literal.

## 2. Logline and arc

A hundred soldiers are dropped 150 million years back for a week. The film weighs each thing that could kill them (air, water, food, giants, predators, exhaustion, germs) and finds the most dangerous thing might be what they carry.

- **Setup** (0:00–1:16): silence, the soldiers, the mission, the place (Morrison Formation map), the question.
- **The world** (1:16–3:36): plants, air, water, food. Quiet dangers. Day 1.
- **The giants** (3:36–4:31): sauropods. Day 2.
- **The hunters** (4:31–8:41): the night camp, Allosaurus, the quarry, weapons, Big Al, the rest of the predators, Stegosaurus.
- **Attrition** (8:41–10:00): exhaustion, then infection, and the turn: the danger runs both ways.
- **Verdict and coda** (10:00–11:02): most survive, at a cost. Day 7: the animals as hills on the floodplain, the soldiers gone, the bones in the rock.

## 3. Benchmark

- **A. M. Cassandre posters** (style grammar): giant geometry, low horizons, airbrush inside hard edges. Learn: one huge shape per frame; scale through perspective. Don't take: any layout.
- **Prehistoric Planet / Walking with Dinosaurs** (topic): animals as real animals, behaviour shown before it is explained. Learn: show the animal *doing* the thing the narrator says (the stego turning, the allosaur stepping out). Don't take: any designs, shots or music.
- **Charles R. Knight's museum murals** (palaeo-art lineage): composed, calm, monumental animals. Learn: the "slow hills" calm of sauropods. Don't take: outdated anatomy.

## 4. Look and cast

- Palette: warm lacquer black, gold, ivory. **Soldiers carry the one accent**: a terracotta desert tan (`#c8774f`), so the eye finds them in any wide shot. The jade jewel tone is for the Jurassic plants and the river. Dinosaurs are deep natural tones (umber, slate, olive, plum), airbrushed, with a gold rim on the lit side.
- Dinosaurs (side profile rigs in metres, walk cycles, jaw, neck and tail motion): Allosaurus (8.5 m), Stegosaurus (9 m), Diplodocus (25 m), Apatosaurus (22 m), Brachiosaurus (22 m, about 12 m tall), Ceratosaurus (6 m), Torvosaurus (10 m), Ornitholestes (2 m). Also lungfish, a small mammal, a lizard and a crocodylomorph.
- Soldiers: helmet, plate carrier, rifle; poses for stand, walk, aim, kneel-aim, sit (exhausted), crouch at the water; an overhead top-down figure for formations of 100.
- Plants: araucarian conifers, tree ferns, cycads, ginkgo, horsetails, ferns. **No grass, no flowers anywhere.**

## 6. Sound

- Voice: the user's recording, used as it is (lightly compressed and levelled, nothing else).
- Score: Gershwin-era symphonic jazz in the style's palette, kept low under the voice (ducked about 10–12 dB). Each chapter has its own state: open fifths and harp for the calm world; a pizzicato bass walk for the survival logistics; low strings and timpani for the hunters; celesta and bowed vibraphone for infection; the full theme at Day 7.
- Foley: wind in ferns, river, insects at night, footfalls for the giants (sub-bass), clicks for counted bulbs, relays and door leaves for transitions, distant rifle fire (kept soft and short), a radio squelch.
- Silences: before "Allosaurus." (night camp), before "Then it turns.", and before "It might be invisible."

## 7. Titles and subtitles

Subtitle card as in `STYLE.md` §4 (lacquer bar, double gold keyline, Josefin Sans SemiBold 40 px ivory), chunked from the word timings, never over a key action. Chapter labels on the DAY medallion and the threat board; names of animals and plants in Josefin Sans caps on gold plaques; numbers in Poiret One. The end card is a title bar: ONE WEEK IN THE JURASSIC.
