# Could You Beat an Ice Age Predator? — treatment

Style: Art Deco (`styles/art-deco/STYLE.md`) in the **bright poster look** the user approved on the Jurassic test: daylight skies, jewel-teal stages, gold line and airbrushed volumes. The user's script and voiceover (ElevenLabs "Peter", 9 min 53 s). The recording stops after "…what its world became."; the script's last two lines become gold title cards over the score. 1920×1080, 24 fps, English, burned-in subtitles + `.srt`, −14 LUFS.

The user asked for three things beyond the last film: **faces**, **a character**, and **smoother transitions**.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **A field guide.** One plate per predator, each with a weight, a skull and a verdict. Clear, but it is the Jurassic film's "spec plate" again, and it has no one to care about.
2. **Sparta's dream.** The frozen cub "remembers" its world. Lovely bookends, but a lion cub can't stand in for "you" in a story that asks whether *you* could win.
3. **The ladder, with You on it (chosen).** A recurring human character, **You** (a modern person in a coral parka with an expressive face), is dropped into the Ice Age and climbs a gold **ziggurat of predators** from "most beatable" to "not even close": dire wolf → cave hyena → Smilodon → Homotherium → cave lion → short-faced bear. Each step ends on a **VERSUS card**: You on the left, the animal on the right, two weight dials, and a verdict plaque. You's face carries the arc: curious, then worried, then afraid, then (when early humans arrive with spears and fire) understanding. Sparta the frozen cub opens and closes the film; the cave painters are the twist.

## 2. Arc

- **Setup** (0:00–1:00): Sparta in the permafrost, then the Ice Age world, early humans, the question, the ladder.
- **Climb** (1:00–8:49): six predators, each with its evidence: La Brea, the DNA result, Kirkdale cave, the healed Smilodon, the Yakutia cub, the Chauvet paintings, the bear's changing reputation. Each ends with You vs it.
- **Turn** (8:49–9:48): "none of them", but humans stayed. Spears, fire, cooperation; the predators switch off one by one on the ladder.
- **Coda** (9:48–10:10): Sparta again; the last lines as title cards on the cave wall, a hand-print in ochre.

## 3. Benchmark

- **A. M. Cassandre travel posters** (bright, monumental, airbrushed). Learn: saturated skies, one huge shape. Don't take: layouts.
- **Disney's 1950s "Toot, Whistle, Plunk and Boom" / UPA character design.** Learn: faces that read in one glance with few lines (brows carry emotion). Don't take: characters.
- **Chauvet Cave paintings** (the real ones are the subject): ochre and charcoal line, animals in groups, manes absent. Learn: the line quality for the painting scenes. Don't take: we redraw in our own line; no tracing.

## 4. Cast

- **You**: a modern adult in a coral parka (the film's one accent colour), dark trousers, dark hair; a front view, profile walk and a close-up face rig with brows, eyes (look direction, blink), mouth shapes, for six expressions: curious, worried, scared, determined, awe, sad.
- **Early humans**: fur clothing in ochre and umber; spear, torch; a painter with charcoal and ochre; faces with the same rig in another palette.
- **Animals** (side-profile rigs in metres, real proportions, walk cycles, faces with eyes, ears, nose and mouth/snarl): dire wolf, cave hyena (spots, sloping back), Smilodon (bobtail, sabres, heavy forelimbs), Homotherium (long forelimbs, sloping back, scimitar teeth), cave lion (no mane, faint stripes), short-faced bear (long legs, short face; standing pose); prey: mammoth, bison, horse, reindeer. Sparta and the Homotherium cub as frozen, curled cubs.

## 5. Transitions (smoother)

No hard cuts and no snap-doors this time. Every change of shot is a 1.0–1.3 s eased move straddling the cut:
- **Gold arch** opening with a feathered edge while the outgoing shot pushes in 6% and the incoming one settles from 110%.
- **Sheen sweep**: a diagonal band of gold light crosses the frame and reveals the next shot behind it.
- **Iris on a shape match**: eye → sun, cub → painted lion, skull → medallion.
- **Camera continuity**: the outgoing shot keeps drifting the way the next one starts.

## 6. Sound

The user's voice as recorded. The score is warmer and brighter than the Jurassic film: harp, celesta, clarinet, strings; low strings and timpani for the big predators; bowed vibraphone for the frozen cubs. Foley: wind on the steppe, snow crunch, bubbling tar, wolf howls (synthesised), hyena whoops, a roar, fire crackle, charcoal on rock. Silences before "No." (Homotherium), before "No." (cave lion) and before "We stayed."

## 7. Titles and subtitles

Subtitle card as in `STYLE.md` §4. The ladder steps carry animal names in Josefin Sans caps; numbers (60 kg, 200 kg, 400 kg, 1 tonne, 3 m) in Poiret One on dials. The ending lines are full-frame title bars.
